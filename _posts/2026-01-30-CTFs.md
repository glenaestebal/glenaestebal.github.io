### 🕵🏻‍♀️ Beginner Friendly CTF Challenges in [TryHackMe](https://tryhackme.com/resources/blog/your-guide-to-beginner-friendly-ctf-challenges-in-2025) 

CTFs simulate real-world cyberattacks in controlled environments. Many challenges are inspired by actual breaches, letting beginners experience how attacks happen without any legal or ethical risk. 

---

## Top Beginner CTF Challenges (2025)

These challenges are widely considered approachable—even for complete beginners:
* [Pickle Rick]({% link _posts/2026-02-03-CTFs-Pickle-Rick.md %}) – Web enumeration and command injection basics
* Basic Pentesting – Network scanning and SSH brute-force concepts
* RootMe – File upload bypass and privilege escalation
* Simple CTF – Reconnaissance and credential discovery
* Bounty Hacker – Sudo misconfigurations and privilege abuse
* LazyAdmin – Directory discovery and CMS exploitation

---

## Core Skills

Beginner-friendly CTFs help develop the exact skills employers look for:
* **Network Scanning & Enumeration** – using tools like Nmap
* **Web Application Testing** – aligned with OWASP principles
* **Linux Command Line Skills** – file navigation, permissions, and privilege escalation
* **Analytical Thinking** – solving problems methodically under pressure

---

## Tips for CTF Success

* **Start Small** – Build confidence with easier challenges first
* **Take Notes** – Record commands, mistakes, and lessons learned
* **Join Communities** – TryHackMe Discord, Reddit, and cybersecurity forums
* **Be Consistent** – One challenge per week beats irregular cramming
* **Use Hints Strategically** – Walkthroughs aren’t cheating if used to understand gaps, research concepts, and retry challenges independently

---

## What Comes After Beginner CTFs?

After completing your first few challenges, you can progress to:

* Competitive CTFs and cyber leagues
* Advanced labs and real-world simulations
* Certification paths such as **SAL1** and **PT1**
* Entry-level roles in penetration testing or security analysis

---

## Final Thoughts

Every cybersecurity professional starts somewhere—and for most, that place is CTFs. The skills you gain through beginner challenges directly translate to real-world defense and offensive security work. With steady practice, you’re building the same foundation used by professionals today.
